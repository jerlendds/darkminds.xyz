---
title: eink constraints
---

How can we reduce information overload through software and digital tools? I think [eink tablets have lots of potential as academic environments](./Writing/eink.md).
I want to explore building eink apps to get away from engagement-hacking tech. Malleable and portable thinking environments that are deliberately slower, quieter, and less capable of demanding attention.

Most software treats information overload as an organizational problem: _better folders_, _better search_, _better recommendations_, _better summaries_. We make consuming information frictionless while making reflection, synthesis, and forgetting unusually difficult. We encounter the same claims in slightly different forms, and confuse recognition with understanding.

What would it mean to design an information environment around cognitive limits rather than engagement? A system might constrain how much new material can enter at once, distinguish between material that has been saved and material that has actually been understood, expose repetition across sources, and periodically ask whether an item still deserves attention. It might optimize for completion, retention, and transfer rather than clicks or time spent.

eink is interesting here partly because of its limitations. Slow refresh rates, reduced color, lower animation fidelity, and weaker multitasking are normally treated as defects. They may instead function as useful constraints. The device can become less like a general-purpose portal and more like a bounded workspace for reading, annotation, recall, and synthesis.

Some questions worth exploring:

- Can software measure the difference between collecting information and learning from it?
- Should reading queues have hard limits, decay functions, or forced prioritization?
- Can repeated claims be clustered so that ten articles do not create the illusion of ten independent pieces of evidence?
- What interaction patterns encourage citation-following, rereading, retrieval, and comparison?
- Can an interface surface when a person's information diet has become too narrow, repetitive, fragmented, or feed-dependent?
- What should an academic operating environment optimize for: comprehension, recall, idea production, completed arguments, or something else?
- How much capability should be deliberately removed from a device intended for serious thought?

The broader question is whether information tools should merely help us manage abundance, or actively govern the rate at which information enters our lives. Perhaps the next useful interface is not one that helps us consume more, but one that makes us more selective about what becomes cognitively present.
